Inside the lesson

What the two hours
actually look like

A class is not a lecture with the camera on. Most of the two hours is spent working, with the tutor watching what the class produces and changing direction when it is clearly not landing.

First
Picking up last week

A short set of questions on what was covered the week before. It takes a few minutes and it tells the tutor what actually stuck, which decides how fast the rest of the class can move.

Then
The new topic, worked

The tutor works through the method on the shared whiteboard, then hands it to the class. Students work the next one themselves while the tutor watches the boards and steps in where something has gone wrong.

Last
Exam-style questions

Questions in the form the exam actually asks them, because knowing a method and recognising when a question is asking for it are two different skills. Marks are lost on the second one far more often than the first.

Work set between classes is short and specific rather than a stack of pages, and the tutor sees what came back before the next class starts. Every exam board we teach to is covered: AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC, CIE and Eduqas.

Honest fit

Who a class suits,
and who it does not

It suits
A child keeping broadly up

A student who follows most of what happens in school but needs the specification covered properly and a fixed weekly slot that makes the work happen. It also suits a household where the cost of weekly one-to-one across a whole year is the thing standing in the way.

It does not suit
A child a long way behind

If your child is several years behind on the foundations, a class moving at GCSE pace will not fix that, and we would rather say so than take the money. Start with one-to-one, find the gap, and move into a class once the ground is solid.

Not sure which describes your child? The free trial lesson is the cheapest way to find out, and a tutor will tell you honestly which of the two they think you need.

Which one

Group class or
one-to-one?

They are not competing products, and one is not a thin version of the other. They answer different problems, and plenty of families use both.

A group class
Steady pace, shared cost

Best when your child is broadly keeping up and needs the specification covered properly, week after week, with the discipline of a fixed slot. Hearing another student ask the question they were too unsure to ask is worth more than it sounds.

One-to-one
The whole hour, one child

Best when something specific is wrong and needs finding. A tutor can spend the whole hour on the gap that is actually causing trouble, which is usually older than the topic your child is stuck on. Rates are on the pricing page.

Questions

Questions parents ask
about group classes

How many children are in a class?

Ten at most, and usually fewer. That is small enough for the tutor to see who is stuck without anyone having to say so, which is the whole point of the format.

Is a group class as good as one-to-one?

They do different jobs. A group class works through the specification at a steady pace with other students, which suits a child who is broadly keeping up. One-to-one is better when there is a specific gap to find and fix, because the whole hour goes on your child.

What happens if we miss a week?

Every class is recorded and the whiteboard is saved, so your child can watch the lesson back and see the same working the class saw. You also get the written note afterwards, the same as any lesson.
