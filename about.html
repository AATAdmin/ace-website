About us

Online tutoring in Maths,
English and Science.

We teach online, one to one and in small groups, from KS1 to A-Level, and we prepare children for the 11+. Lessons are taught by subject tutors, and after every one you get a written report on what was covered and what to work on next.

Who we are

The company

Ace Academic Tutors Ltd was founded in 2024 by Hamza Liaquet, who holds a PhD in fluid dynamics, and Anum Liaquet, a secondary maths teacher.

We are mathematicians and subject specialists who teach. The classroom, the lesson reports and the progress tracking were all built in-house rather than bought in, so that we could give parents a straighter answer about how their child is doing.

In the lesson

How lessons run

A shared whiteboard

Lessons are live, with video and a whiteboard the students write on too. Working gets written out rather than described.

Past papers, opened together

Screens can be shared, and a past paper or worksheet can be opened and marked inside the lesson.

Phone, tablet or laptop

Each has its own layout, so a lesson taken on a phone works the same as one taken on a laptop.

In their own time

Between the lessons

A lesson is an hour. Most of the week is not, so the tools a student uses on their own are part of the same picture rather than something separate.

Homework, set on the lesson

The tutor sets practice on what was actually covered that day, so the week has something specific in it rather than a general instruction to revise.

Practice they choose themselves

A student can work on any topic in their subject, not only the ones their tutor has reached. Useful for getting ahead, and for going back to something from last year that never quite made sense.

The tutor sees all of it

What was attempted, what went wrong and when. The next lesson can start from that rather than from asking how the week went.

Work done alone counts the same as work done in a lesson. The topic picture moves either way, so a child who puts the hours in on their own gets the credit for it.

Afterwards

What every parent gets

The same four things, for every student and every tutor, and none of it is something you have to ask for.

A written report after every lesson

A few paragraphs, not a tick box. Which topics were covered, which questions your child got right and which they did not, what was set as homework, and what the next lesson will start with.

Every report kept, in order

You can read back across a term in one place. That is how you spot the weakness that keeps reappearing, rather than reading each lesson on its own and forgetting the last one.

The topic picture, kept up to date

Which topics have moved and which have not been touched since September. If nothing has shifted in six weeks, you can see that and ask about it.

Lessons, attendance and payments

The schedule, what was taken, what was missed and what was rescheduled, taken from who was actually in the room. Invoices and what you have paid sit in the same place.

Progress

How we know what
your child can do

“Algebra” is not one thing. Underneath it sit expanding brackets, factorising, solving quadratics, completing the square, and a dozen more. A child can be fine with the first two and stuck on the fourth, and a single label like “algebra” hides exactly that.

So we break each subject down the way the national curriculum does, into topics and then into the individual things a child is expected to be able to do. GCSE maths comes to thirteen topics. Each one gets a plain description of where your child is at the moment.

Not yet
Has not managed these questions yet, or has not tried them.
Emerging
Gets there on the easier questions and comes unstuck when they get harder.
Secure
Can do it reliably at the level they are working at, not just once.
Confident
Still does it weeks later, and on the harder exam-style questions.

One right answer does not move a topic. Practice keeps going back to whatever is weakest, and returns to things a few weeks after they looked fine, because a topic that is dropped in October is usually the one that goes wrong in the exam.

Homework is marked as it is done, but anything we are not confident about is set aside for the tutor to look at rather than counted automatically.

Tutors

How we choose tutors

We teach Maths, English and Science, and we prepare children for the 11+. A tutor teaches their own subject and no others. Tutors are university-educated, with degree-level knowledge of the subject they teach.

  • Enhanced DBSHeld before a first lesson, with no exceptions. We can produce it on request.

  • Reports are readLesson reports are read while a tutor is teaching, not at the end of a term.
  • Lessons are observedSessions are observed from time to time as part of how tutors are supported.
If it is not working

Changing tutor, and
raising a concern

If a tutor is not the right fit for your child, tell us and we will change them. There is no charge for it, you do not need to give a reason, and it happens often enough that nobody will find it awkward.

Call or email, say you would like someone else, and we arrange the next lesson with a different tutor. Nothing else about your booking changes.

Anything that worries you goes the same way, to a person rather than a form. Our safeguarding policy sets out how we handle concerns about a child's welfare, and who deals with them.
